# Vowvel — invitation preview redesign

## Scope and reference reading

Preserve the approved landing page, brand, collection names, and editor data. Redesign only the guest invitation worlds and their opening experience. Contact sheets reviewed: `docs/references/reels/reel-1.jpg`, `reel-2.jpg`, `reel-3.jpg`. These are selected stills; timing below is a new design proposal, not measured reference timing.

The strongest reference pattern is a **portrait invitation object that becomes a portrait celebration**. Reel 1 shows a nearly full-screen blush envelope and gold seal becoming an immersive framed couple-name scene. Reel 2 pairs a warm opening with a burgundy title and clean ivory letter below. Reel 3 carries one dark, ornate visual language from opening through RSVP. All three move through a compact sequence: invitation → personal note → scratch → photograph → countdown → program → venue → useful details → reply.

Our first previews felt like five long websites: large landscape images, generic two-column editorial sections, repeated gallery decoration, and a small envelope floating above the page. The redesign must feel like five commissioned invitation artworks. Text must be composed inside intentional negative space from the beginning; never repair busy artwork by washing out the entire image.

## Shared construction rules

- Use a portrait stage around 430–500px wide; on desktop show the complete artwork centered in a calm dark/neutral surround. On phone it fills the width naturally. Guest pages do not become desktop marketing layouts.
- Design each cover around a real 9:16 composition. Couple names, occasion, and date fit inside the first opened viewport at 390×844. Keep main names roughly 52–72px depending on length, not desktop-sized.
- Envelope/folio fills most of the initial stage. The envelope's surface is the opening screen; no second oversized heading above it. Show names and date as actual HTML printed onto the object.
- Each opening moves into its corresponding hero with matched texture, framing, and camera; no unrelated background replacement. Use separate layers for flap, cover, shadow, inner scene, and text.
- Keep a small “View invitation” bypass and accessible one-tap opening. Motion sequence completes in 1.6–2.4s; reduced motion opens instantly. No looping falling petals behind long text.
- Following sections are part of one continuous paper/velvet/painted scroll. Small illustrative transitions, consistent thin rules, selected ornament and meaningful spacing provide continuity.
- Date is present outside the scratch card. Scratch is a tactile secondary keepsake: a phrase, the couple's date again, or personal note. Real erasure; keyboard reveal; no blocked essential information.
- No stock app icons, floating bottom pill overlapping the artwork, repeated background crops presented as photographs, oversized empty gaps, or hover-only interactions. A compact in-flow RSVP/Directions/Calendar row can recur after the title and venue.
- Calendar, map, local preview RSVP, draft persistence, and honest payment boundaries remain functional. Keep copy legible at 14–16px, body line length around 30–42 characters.

## 01 · The Conservatory / a pressed-flower letter

**Material:** pale sage handmade paper, translucent vellum, ivory wildflowers, faded olive botanical ink. Warm and intimate, with rich tactile close-ups rather than a landscape garden.

**Cover:** full portrait envelope with asymmetrical folded vellum corners. Pressed stems are visible through its side edges. Small cream wax seal at the fold; names printed along the lower third.

**Open choreography (1.9s):** seal releases 0–180ms → triangular flap rotates back 180–750ms with changing shadow → inner letter rises 450–1200ms → side botanical layers spread 900–1500ms → names resolve on ivory insert 1200–1900ms. The final frame is already the live hero, so there is no additional fade-to-page.

**Opened artwork:** narrow botanical frame climbing the left edge and one right upper corner; centred names and a small oval monogram, quiet irregular deckle bottom. Scene uses three original transparent botanical pieces, not a full image behind text.

**Scroll:** formal note → pale gold botanical scratch stamp → one tipped portrait/photo-free illustrated date keepsake → slim vertical day counter → ceremony botanical labels → venue herbarium card → travel accordion → RSVP reply paper.

**Signature:** the scratch stamp reveals a miniature pressed blossom and personal note. The ceremony rule extends like a stem, with two small leaf nodes.

## 02 · Gulmohar / the illustrated royal folio

**Material:** saturated vermilion, warm sandstone, hand-painted indigo/green detail, aged brass. Indian miniature craftsmanship, with original architecture rather than photographs of a claimed venue.

**Cover:** full-height two-door gatefold with a symmetrical hand-painted palace facade. The central seam and brass medallion are real layers. Names appear as modest ink on a lower paper cartouche.

**Open choreography (2.2s):** medallion separates 0–200ms → both gatefolds swing outward from vertical hinges 150–1200ms → courtyard depth layers move 12–25px 550–1600ms → interior parchment arch/title comes forward 1100–1900ms → tiny border light settles 1900–2200ms.

**Opened artwork:** a high arch with slender painted columns, names within a clean sandstone recess, tiny garden foreground. The title frame looks constructed around its content, not an opaque oval pasted on top of a scenic image.

**Scroll:** family blessing → date medallion/scratch → couple portrait in a scalloped frame → four small countdown niches → ceremony procession with miniature symbols → illustrated venue cartouche → attire and stay inserts → bordered RSVP folio.

**Signature:** entire scroll retains fine miniature borders and sand-colored writing surface, punctuated by vermilion ceremony panels. Keep decorative framing optically thin enough for generous text width.

## 03 · After Hours / the velvet invitation

**Material:** almost-black burgundy velvet, engraved muted brass, warm candle bokeh only in the far perimeter. A private supper club, genuinely elegant rather than a generic dark theme.

**Cover:** an imposing portrait black-cherry sleeve with geometric brass corner inlays and a small vertical ticket seam. “An evening for” sits above restrained engraved couple initials.

**Open choreography (1.8s):** central clasp depresses 0–150ms → sleeve halves retract left/right 150–1000ms → tall inner title card moves forward with a small perspective correction 550–1300ms → engraved lines draw in two coordinated strokes 900–1500ms → names gain opacity 1200–1800ms.

**Opened artwork:** full-length deco frame, vertically stacked names, tiny diamond separators, symmetrical minimal brass tracery. Maintain dark material through the whole page, as reel 3 does; no sudden beige sections.

**Scroll:** intimate greeting → brushed-brass scratch ticket → a single cinematic portrait → split-flap inspired static countdown numerals → chaptered evening program → venue invitation card → dress note → RSVP guest-list card.

**Signature:** events form a typographic evening program, not boxes; fine aligned time column and stage-like act titles. One moving glint on opening only, no persistent metallic shimmer.

## 04 · The Sunday Edit / the handmade memory box

**Material:** butter-yellow paper, cobalt ink, cherry-red pencil, perforated photo edges, cloth tape. Original hand-built craft with adult typographic discipline.

**Cover:** portrait paper pocket, large off-centre address label, one illustrated stamp and a visible cobalt pull-tab. Small personal doodles are sparse; names are on the label, not above the artwork.

**Open choreography (2.1s):** tab shifts down 0–200ms → paper pocket compresses 200–600ms → three accordion folds unfurl upward in sequence 400–1400ms → clipped photos/illustrations settle 1000–1750ms → name letters settle into their already readable positions 1500–2100ms. No exaggerated bounce.

**Opened artwork:** playful photo-booth strip down one edge, oversized serif names in an intentional cream rectangle, small note “we're doing this!” and a hand-drawn underline. On narrow screens every decoration has reserved space; no stickers cover date or controls.

**Scroll:** short personal note → scratch postage label → genuine photos in a contact strip (or one original illustrated keepsake) → detachable calendar block → accordion ceremony strip → venue postcard → packing/dress notes → RSVP stamped reply card.

**Signature:** program folds are paper panels with alternating subtle rotations; a visible thread visually joins chapters. Tone is affectionate and concise, not a page of puns.

## 05 · Azure / the coastal postcard

**Material:** luminous cotton white, cobalt watercolor, pale terracotta, fine maritime pen lines, softly embossed postage. Destination romance with daylight and breathing room.

**Cover:** portrait folded airmail letter, alternating thin blue/red edge marks, circular postmark, sea illustration glimpsed along the top fold. The first screen is a physical letter, not a landscape with floating names.

**Open choreography (2.0s):** postage seal releases 0–180ms → folded paper rotates open downward 180–900ms → illustrated upper panel lifts into a three-layer coastal diorama 650–1450ms → live names emerge in the clear sky/white center 1100–1800ms → postmark eases to its final corner 1500–2000ms.

**Opened artwork:** watercolor coastal frame lives in the top and lower quarter; clear middle paper band holds names/date. One painted balcony edge adds depth. Never put small type over cliffs, trees, or sea texture.

**Scroll:** letter from the couple → postcard scratch stamp → one landscape memory → nautical date notation/countdown → itinerary as dated postcards → venue address/map action → travel and stay guide → correspondence-style RSVP.

**Signature:** each event has a different small original coastal vignette/line illustration. Keep informative travel detail on plain paper instead of overlaying the scene.

## Asset brief and implementation order

Produce a cohesive portrait cover + opened scene for each world. Reserve text space, generate no baked-in names. Prefer 2–4 reusable transparent/flat layers per design so the cover physically transforms into the final scene. Existing landing artwork stays unchanged.

1. Implement Conservatory portrait stage and match its open/closed composition; verify 390×844 before applying any shared section primitives.
2. Implement the other four unique mechanisms and hero arrangements; they must be distinguishable even in grayscale.
3. Integrate continuous theme-specific sections, photos, scratch, countdown, and reply using the existing data model.
4. Test all five mobile title screens for visible names/date, typography contrast, no blocked controls, and stable layout through opening. Test opening bypass, reduced motion, route-safe internal navigation, and all six uploaded photos.
5. Only then capture any video first/last frames: camera, paper geometry, and framing must match exactly. Video is progressive enhancement; live HTML supplies personalized names and practical details.

**Success test:** a screenshot of any opened preview should look like an invitation someone commissioned, even with no browser context. Its opening should feel like receiving that exact object. The approved landing remains unchanged.
